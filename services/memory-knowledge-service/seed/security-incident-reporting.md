# Reporting a security incident

## What counts as a security incident

Report anything that could expose company data or accounts: a lost or stolen laptop or phone, a clicked phishing link, a password entered on a fake page, malware warnings, an account acting on its own, or sensitive data sent to the wrong person.

## How to report an incident quickly

Contact the service desk immediately and say it is a security incident so it is prioritized. If you cannot use your computer, phone the support line. Reporting fast and honestly is always the right move; you will not be blamed for reporting.

## Lost or stolen device

If a laptop or phone is lost or stolen, report it right away so it can be remotely locked and wiped and its access revoked. Then file a brief summary of when and where it went missing. A police report may be needed for insurance on a stolen device.

## Suspected malware or ransomware

If you see ransom messages, files being renamed or encrypted, or unexpected pop‑ups, disconnect the machine from Wi‑Fi and wired network immediately but leave it powered on, and call the support line. Do not try to clean it yourself.

## Accidental data disclosure

If you sent Confidential or Restricted data to the wrong person or posted it somewhere public, do not try to quietly recall it and move on. Report it so the exposure can be assessed and contained; timing affects how serious the impact is.
